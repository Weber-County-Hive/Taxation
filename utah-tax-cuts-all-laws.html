Bills that didn't pass, or aren't confirmed yet

These are kept so you can compare what was proposed with what passed. They are not in the count above.

Show the 33 bills and their status
  • H.B. 291 (2016) · Working Families Employment Amendments — House passed 65-4-6; no Senate passage. Filed 3/10/2016.. Let GOED consider whether a project offers 'working parent benefits,' favors veteran hiring, or has strategic state benefit when setting the maximum amount and duration of an economic development tax credit.
  • S.B. 178 (2016) · Economic Development Revisions — Did not pass (per DatacenterDynamics: 'quietly killed' in the House); returned as S.B. 3002 in the 2016 Third Special Session. Would have created sales and use tax exemptions for a 'qualifying enterprise data center' (new, 150,000+ sq ft, on 50+ acres). Passed the Senate but did not pass the House in the 2016 General Session.
  • H.B. 453 (2018) · Hydrogen Fuel Production Incentives Amendments — Enacting clause struck; filed 3/8/2018. No final floor passage.. Proposed adding hydrogen fuel production and fueling stations to the High Cost Infrastructure Development Tax Credit project definition.
  • S.J.R. 2 (2018) · Proposal to Amend Utah Constitution - Property Tax Exemptions — Passed Legislature; rejected as 2018 Amendment B. Official canvass: 287,329 for (28.4%), 725,194 against (71.6%).. Proposed adding a constitutional property tax exemption, granted as provided by statute, for real property that the state or a local government entity leases from a private owner. It went on the 2018 ballot as Constitutional Amendment B.
  • S.B. 2001 (2019) · Tax Restructuring (2019 Third/December special session) — Repealed (H.B. 185, January 2020, after a referendum petition). Cut the income tax rate from 4.95% to 4.66% and added a grocery credit, while raising the sales tax on food from 1.75% to 4.85% and extending sales tax to gas and some services.
  • S.B. 241 (2020) · Rural Economic Development Amendments — not confirmed (likely did not pass; similar policy enacted as H.B. 356 in 2021). Changed how GOED may authorize tax credits for new commercial projects in 3rd- through 6th-class counties and revised the definitions of 'new commercial project,' 'high paying job' and 'significant capital investment' for those counties.
  • H.B. 165 (2022) · Food Sales Tax Amendments — Filed in House file for bills not passed. Would remove state tax on food and food ingredients while taxing candy at the general rate.
  • H.B. 187 (2022) · Agricultural Assessment Amendments — Enacting clause struck; filed 3/4/2022. First substitute was not adopted.. Would have exempted from rollback tax the part of land withdrawn from Farmland Assessment Act treatment that qualifies for urban farming assessment, and created an urban farming renewal application.
  • H.B. 401 (2022) · Property Tax Relief Amendments — Did not pass. Would have raised circuit breaker income caps and credits.
  • H.B. 478 (2022) · Minimum Basic Tax Rate Reduction — Did not pass. Would have repealed the Equity Pupil Tax Rate and the WPU Value Tax Rate parts of the statewide basic school levy, avoiding an expected statewide property tax increase.
  • H.B. 101 (2023) · Food Sales Tax Amendments — Did not pass separately; content folded into H.B. 54. Would have removed the state sales tax on food. It passed the House but was held once its content went into H.B. 54.
  • H.B. 172 (2023) · Food Sales Tax Modifications — Filed in House file for bills not passed. Would remove state tax on food and food ingredients while taxing candy at the general rate.
  • H.B. 240 (2023) · Income Tax Amendments — Unknown; likely did not pass (H.B. 54 was the vehicle that passed). Proposed an income tax rate reduction. The new rate is not stated in the fiscal note.
  • H.B. 260 (2023) · Property Tax Income Requirements — Did not pass. Would have raised circuit breaker income eligibility to $50,000 for homeowners and renters (per UIJP), expanded the credits and the 20% valuation reduction, and broadened renter's credit income criteria.
  • H.B. 296 (2023) · Minimum Basic Tax Rate Reduction — Unknown (not confirmed; likely did not pass). Proposed lowering the statewide basic school levy by $100 million a year and blocking a $22.9 million WPU Value Rate increase, with the Uniform School Fund replacing the revenue.
  • H.B. 557 (2023) · Tax Increment Financing Revisions — not confirmed. Added reporting requirements and penalties for agencies using tax increment, and required project area plans to explain why TIF is needed; added private-investment feasibility to GOEO credit agreement factors.
  • H.B. 88 (2023) · Social Security Tax Amendments — Content folded into H.B. 54 (per news); standalone bill outcome not confirmed. Expanded eligibility for the Social Security benefits tax credit. Per St. George News, this content was rolled into H.B. 54.
  • S.B. 141 (2023) · Reduction of Minimum Basic Tax Rate — Did not pass (filed in Senate file for bills not passed 3/3/2023). Proposed lowering the statewide basic school levy by $128 million a year and blocking a $22.9 million WPU Value Rate increase.
  • S.B. 235 (2023) · Tax Amendments — not confirmed. Created a sales tax exemption for sand, gravel, rock aggregate or cement products sold between entities under 100% common ownership, and a rolling stock exemption.
  • S.J.R. 1 (2023) · Proposal to Amend Utah Constitution - Revenue and Taxation — Did not pass. Would have added to Article XIII, Section 3 a ban on the state and its political subdivisions imposing a tax on the transfer of real property.
  • H.B. 136 (2024) · Social Security Tax Amendments — Unknown; no votes on the Utah Taxpayers Association list; likely did not pass (the expansion came in 2025 S.B. 71). Proposed increasing the Social Security benefits income tax credit.
  • H.B. 149 (2024) · Earned Income Tax Credit Amendments — Unknown; no votes on the Utah Taxpayers Association list; likely did not pass. Proposed making the state EITC refundable.
  • H.B. 326 (2024) · Firearm Safety Device Tax Incentive Amendments — Unknown. Proposed a nonrefundable individual income tax credit for buying a firearm safety device.
  • H.B. 510 (2024) · Income Tax Age Requirements — Unknown. Proposed exempting minors from state income tax.
  • H.B. 512 (2024) · Minimum Basic Tax Rate Reduction — Did not pass. Proposed lowering the statewide basic school levy by $75 million a year and blocking a $27.9 million WPU Value Rate increase.
  • H.B. 557 (2024) · Income Tax Deduction Amendments — not confirmed (likely did not pass; no Senate sponsor on text). Would have created corporate and individual income tax deductions for bonus depreciation on certain assets.
  • S.B. 103 (2024) · Military Tax Credit — Unknown. Proposed a tax credit for military reserve members on their stipend.
  • S.B. 146 (2024) · Property Tax Offset Amendments — Unknown (no floor votes listed; likely did not pass). Would authorize counties to let people over 65 or with disabilities offset property tax by doing county work (Taxpayers Assn description).
  • S.B. 71 (2024) · Cannabis Business Tax Credit Amendments — Did not pass (House struck enacting clause; filed 3/1/2024). Would have amended a nonrefundable income tax credit for businesses selling medical cannabis.
  • S.J.R. 2 (2024) · Proposal to Amend Utah Constitution - Prohibition on Real Estate Transfer Tax — Did not pass (struck/filed 3/1/2024). Would have barred imposing or authorizing a new tax or fee on the transfer of real property.
  • S.B. 85 (2025) · Income Tax Rate Amendments (1st Substitute) — Unknown; rate tables show no automatic trigger in effect. Proposed automatic yearly rate cuts from 2027 to 2035 whenever actual revenue beats the forecast, returning half the excess to taxpayers, then fixing the rate after 2035.
  • H.B. 124 (2026) · Veteran Armed Forces Property Tax Exemption Amendments — Did not pass. Would have amended the veteran armed forces property tax exemption; the specific provisions were not described in the sources opened.
  • S.B. 223 (2026) · Sales and Use Tax Exemptions Modifications — Failed (House struck enacting clause 3/6/2026). Energy storage-related sales tax exemption changes; passed Senate 24-0 but House struck the enacting clause.
