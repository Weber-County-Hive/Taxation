From 2015 through 2026, our inventory holds 135 laws that changed Utah taxes or fees. Each law is counted once.

Other rule changes

Counting laws is not counting dollars. The five income tax rate cuts are most of the stated dollars. See who gets the relief, and how much.
This count is a floor. It holds only laws we have confirmed. Bills that did not pass and possible bills we could not yet confirm are kept on the full-list pages, labeled, and are not in these numbers.

Cut and raised in the same bill

These 9 laws lowered one tax and raised another in the same bill. Each line comes from the bill's fiscal note or bill text unless it says otherwise.

H.B. 31 (2016) · Enterprise Zone Amendments

Primary sponsor · floor sponsor: Rep. Scott Sandall · Sen. Ralph Okerlund
LoweredOpened new areas to enterprise zone credits (Box Elder County, Tooele and Brigham City).
RaisedRepealed the 50% enterprise zone income tax credit for cash gifts to private nonprofits.
Fiscal note: repeal +$585,300 a year to the Education Fund (LFA); new zone eligibility up to about $1.16 million a year in revenue not collected.

S.B. 2001 (2018) · Online Sales Tax Amendments (Second Special Session)

Primary sponsor · floor sponsor: Sen. Curtis Bramble · Rep. Steve Eliason
LoweredExempted manufacturing, mining and lab inputs from sales tax by ending the three-year "useful life" test.
RaisedRequired online sellers with more than $100,000 in Utah sales or 200 sales to collect the sales tax buyers already owed, and ended their 18% vendor discount.
News coverage (Cedar City News): about $60 million a year expected from online sellers, about $55 million of it already set aside for the manufacturing exemption. Fiscal note not yet opened.

H.B. 293 (2018) · Tax Rebalancing Revisions

Primary sponsor · floor sponsor: Rep. Bradley Last · Sen. Lincoln Fillmore
LoweredCut the individual and corporate income tax rate from 5.00% to 4.95%, and phased in single sales factor for some businesses.
RaisedHeld the statewide basic school property tax rate for five years instead of letting it fall, and created a new "WPU Value" property tax rate.
Fiscal note: Education Fund −$52.9 million in FY2019 from the rate cut; property tax +$36.1 million in FY2019 from the rate hold (rising to $125.6 million by FY2023) and +$18.6 million from the WPU Value rate.

S.B. 269 (2019) · Military Development Authority

Primary sponsor · floor sponsor: Sen. Jerry Stevenson · Rep. Val Peterson
LoweredMade hotels, hotel condo units and commercial condo units owned by the Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA) exempt from property tax and privilege tax, even when a private company runs them.
RaisedLet MIDA keep up to 75% of the property tax growth in its project areas for up to 25 years per parcel, plus up to 15 more years if its board finds "significant benefit." Parcels without an occupancy certificate pay MIDA 1.2% a year on value above the base.
Bill text (1st Sub., the version that passed). Same bill: challenges to a project area must be filed within 30 days, and challenges to project areas created before Dec. 1, 2018 are barred. Senate 29-0-0 under suspended rules the night the substitute was adopted; House 67-0-8. Fiscal note amount not yet read.

H.B. 186 (2022) · Vehicle Registration Amendments

Primary sponsor · floor sponsor: Rep. Raymond Ward · Sen. Wayne Harper
LoweredThe fiscal note shows one-time reductions that left the bill's net change below zero.
RaisedRaised electric-vehicle registration from $120 to $130.25 (2023), $180 (2026) and $240 (2032); plug-in hybrids $52 to $56.50; hybrids $20 to $21.75; indexed to inflation.
Fiscal note (2nd Sub.): +$2,516,100 a year to the Transportation Fund from the higher fees; net −$112,800 in FY2023 after one-time reductions.

H.B. 301 (2023) · Transportation Tax Amendments

Primary sponsor · floor sponsor: Rep. Mike Schultz · Sen. Wayne Harper
LoweredCut the gas tax rate from 16.5% to 14.2% of the average wholesale price.
RaisedAdded $7 to most vehicle registrations (indexed to inflation) and created a new 12.5% tax on electricity sold at EV charging stations.
Fiscal note (3rd Sub.): state tax revenue −$32.7 million in FY2024. The note says the gas tax rate could rise to as much as 42 cents a gallon in 2028 or later.

S.B. 156 (2024) · Tax Modifications

Primary sponsor · floor sponsor: Sen. Michael McKell · Rep. Carl Albrecht
LoweredLet radioactive waste facility taxes count toward the High Cost Infrastructure Development Tax Credit.
RaisedCreated a new 0.5% tax rate on certain low-concentration class A radioactive waste.
Still being checked: whether the new 0.5% rate is higher or lower than the rate that would otherwise apply.

H.B. 575 (2026) · Fuel Tax and Supply Amendments

Primary sponsor · floor sponsor: Rep. Calvin Roberts · Sen. Brady Brammer
LoweredSet the gas tax at a flat $0.319 a gallon from July 1 to Dec. 31, 2026, and expanded the High Cost Infrastructure Development Tax Credit to fuel pipelines and salt-cavern storage.
RaisedRaised the alternative fuel tax from 16.5 cents to 21.2 cents per gallon equivalent.
Fiscal note (1st Sub.): −$40,079,700 one-time in FY2027, mostly from the gas tax cut.

H.B. 77 (2026) · Tax Modifications

Primary sponsor · floor sponsor: Rep. Steve Eliason · Sen. Daniel McCay
LoweredRepealed estate transfer taxes.
RaisedAdded 1.5% to the short-term vehicle rental tax (for the Utah Fairpark district) and repealed the cleaner-burning fuel credit.
Fiscal note: Income Tax Fund about +$4 million a year from FY2027. The same bill set different value-jump review triggers: 150% for homes, 350% for other property.

S.B. 2001 (2019 special session) · Tax Restructuring Revisions

Repealed before it took effect
LoweredIncome tax rate from 4.95% to 4.66%, with a grocery tax credit.
RaisedSales tax on food from 1.75% to 4.85%, plus tax on gas and some services.
Repealed by H.B. 185 in January 2020, after a citizen referendum petition. Not counted in the 135 laws.

Key figures

State income tax rate: 4.95% in 2018, 4.45% in 2026. Five rate cuts from 2022 to 2026 (S.B. 59, H.B. 54, S.B. 69, H.B. 106, S.B. 60). The corporate rate matches.
Bill fiscal notes; Utah House Majority, "Utah's Tax Cut Streak"
2026 local property tax increases: $96,055,660 from 68 local entities through Truth in Taxation. Elected councils, commissions and boards voted on them after public hearings.
Utah Taxpayers Association 2026 list (via Utah News Dispatch)
Statewide public infrastructure district (PID) debt: $5,267,000,000 as of Aug. 7, 2026, up from just over $4 billion in fall 2025. Property owners inside each district repay it through district assessments.
State Auditor Tina Cannon, as reported in Cheap on Paper
