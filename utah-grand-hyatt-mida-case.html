The Grand Hyatt Deer Valley: A MIDA-Owned Hotel, Its Tax Bill and Its Lease

State law lets hotels owned by the Military Installation Development Authority skip property tax, even when a private company runs them. Here is what Wasatch County's tax records, MIDA's own resolutions and state tax reports show about the Grand Hyatt.

The short version

The hotel pays no property tax. Wasatch County lists the Grand Hyatt Deer Valley's hotel space (parcel 00-0021-5105, 1.93 acres) under the Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA), a state authority. The county gives it a value of $0, and its tax was $0 in 2025 and 2026.
The homes in the same building do. The 55 privately owned residences in the same condo building have a combined 2026 value of $173,820,235 and owe about $1,769,660 in 2026 property tax. The typical (median) unit owes $26,742.10.
The state law behind it. S.B. 269 (2019) says hotels and hotel or commercial condo units that MIDA owns pay no property tax or privilege tax, even when a private company runs them. Primary sponsor: Sen. Jerry W. Stevenson.
MIDA leases the hotel to a private company. MIDA's board approved a restated lease of the hotel to BLX MWR Hotel LLC on Dec. 15, 2022. That company lists its headquarters at 805 Third Avenue in New York, the address of Extell Development, the company that built the hotel. The rent and the lease terms were not posted with the resolution.
MIDA collects the hotel's room tax. The Utah State Tax Commission sent MIDA $3,398,195.36 from the "MWR Hotel" line in its monthly room tax reports from December 2025 through September 2026.
What the public gets. 100 of the hotel's 381 rooms are set aside for eligible military guests at about $99 to $148 a night, depending on rank. Public rates start around $900 a night in peak ski season.

Where it is

The hotel and MIDA's workforce condos sit in MIDA's Military Recreation Facility project area, at Deer Valley's East Village in Wasatch County, near the Jordanelle Reservoir. Points are the county's parcel locations.

MIDA bought the 42 Pioche Village condos for workforce housing. They are taxed like private property. S.B. 269's exemption covers hotels and hotel or commercial condo units, not housing. Roads and marina lots owned by MIDA districts carry no value, as public property usually does.

Who runs it

The leaseMIDA leases "certain portions of the MWR Hotel" to BLX MWR Hotel LLC. MIDA Resolution 2022-17, passed Dec. 15, 2022 and signed by MIDA Chair J. Stuart Adams, approved an "Amended and Restated MWR Hotel Condominium Lease Agreement." The agreement was not attached to the posted resolution. A memorandum of the lease is recorded with the Wasatch County Recorder as Entry No. 483155.
The companyBLX MWR Hotel LLC is a Delaware company formed Oct. 7, 2019, with headquarters at 805 Third Avenue, 7th Floor, New York. That is the headquarters address of Extell Development, the New York company led by Gary Barnett that built the hotel and donated the site.
The investor fundAn SEC filing for BLX MWR Fund LLC, at the same address, lists Gershon Barnett, BLX MWR Hotel LLC, BLX MWR Residential LLC and Extell Utah Regional Center LLC as related persons. It reported $8 million raised from 10 investors as of Aug. 29, 2023, toward a $75.2 million offering, with an $800,000 minimum.
The brandThe hotel carries the Grand Hyatt name. Hyatt's opening release names Extell as the village developer. No record we read names the day-to-day operator.
Not foundThe rent MIDA receives, how long the lease runs, and who keeps the hotel's operating profits.

The law and the votes behind it

S.B. 269 (2019), "Military Development Authority"Primary sponsor Sen. Jerry W. Stevenson; floor sponsor Rep. Val L. Peterson. Senate 29-0-0 (Mar. 11, 2019, under suspended rules, the same night it was substituted); House 67-0-8 (Mar. 14, 2019). Effective May 14, 2019. Now Utah Code 63H-1-501(7). The fiscal note does not mention the hotel exemption.
MIDA Resolution 19-22 (Oct. 1, 2019)"Extending the Property Tax Allocation for the MWR Hotel" by 15 years, based on a board finding of significant benefit.
MIDA bonds, June 2021The board approved up to $260 million, including about $115 million for the military conference hotel, repaid from tax growth in the project area; Extell's Ex Utah Development guaranteed the bonds (Park Record, June 3, 2021).
MIDA bonds, Oct. 2024Up to $390 million more. The project list includes "MWR Conference Hotel Reimbursement" (TownLift, Dec. 2024).
MIDA Resolution 2022-17 (Dec. 15, 2022)Restated the hotel lease with BLX MWR Hotel LLC.

What the public gets

Military rooms. 100 rooms at a preferred rate for active duty, Guard and Reserve, retirees and Defense Department civilians, plus a military lounge and lift tickets up to 75% off (MIDA). MIDA reported 3,900 discounted room nights at an average of $149 as of June 2026.
MIDA's position on the bonds. MIDA's project area page says the bonds are repaid from tax growth in the project area and that "no other entity – Wasatch County, MIDA nor the State of Utah – will be on the hook for debt service."
Room tax to MIDA. $3,398,195.36 over ten monthly Tax Commission reports. Hill Air Force Base says a tax on regular guests goes to the base's MWR account. The reports do not say how MIDA spends the money.

What the records do not show

  • What the hotel would be worth, and owe, if it were taxed. The county gives the hotel space a $0 value, so the yearly value of the exemption is not known. A home's value per acre should not be applied to a hotel.
  • The rent, length and profit terms of the lease with BLX MWR Hotel LLC.
  • Who manages the hotel day to day.
  • Why MIDA's commercial units carry a flat $24,000 value.
  • How MIDA spends the hotel's room tax.
