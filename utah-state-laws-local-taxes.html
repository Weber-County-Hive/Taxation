State Laws, Local Taxes: Who Used the Power, and What It Cost You

The Legislature writes the rules. Cities, counties, school boards, special districts and state authorities decide whether to use them. Here is which laws gave which power, who decides, and whether voters get a say.

The short version

The state lowered its own taxes and handed out taxing power at the same time. From 2015 to 2026, our inventory counts 31 laws that let a local body or state authority start a tax, levy a new property tax, or keep the growth in property taxes inside a zone. For 27 of them, the law requires no public vote, as far as the text we read shows.
Local bodies used those powers. Salt Lake City adopted a 0.5% prison host-city sales tax in 2018 and a 0.5% Delta Center sales tax in 2024. Weber County adopted a 0.2% sales tax in June 2026, and Davis County adopted the same tax in September, starting Jan. 1, 2027. Each was a council or commission vote, with no ballot question.
Property tax increases are separate. In 2026, 68 local taxing bodies across Utah proposed Truth in Taxation increases totaling $96,055,660 a year, by the Utah Taxpayers Association's list. Cities and towns asked for about half; school districts for about 42%.
What the records don't show: a record tying any of these local increases to the state's income tax cuts. Each local body gave its own reasons. We put them side by side; we don't say one caused the other.

How it works

The Legislature passes a lawIt sets what a local body may tax, how much, and whether voters must approve
▼
A local body decides whether to use itA city council, county commission, school board, district board or state authority board
▼
1. A new local sales or excise taxPaid at the register or on a utility bill. No individual notice to each household. A public vote only if the law requires one.
2. A property tax increaseAbove the certified rate, the body must mail notice and hold a Truth in Taxation hearing. Voters can only reverse it by referendum petition.
3. Tax growth sent to a zoneA share of future property tax growth goes to a zone, PID, CRA or authority instead of the county, schools and districts. No rate goes up, so no Truth in Taxation notice.
▼
You pay

Path 3 doesn't raise anyone's rate. It sends growth that the county, school district and other bodies would otherwise receive to a project. Those bodies still serve the new growth, so the cost can show up later as a rate increase or as less service. The records reviewed don't measure that for any one project.

State rules that move costs to local taxpayers

Every local taxing body sets its rate to collect a budgeted amount. When the state exempts some property, that property leaves the tax base, and the same budget is spread over everyone else's property unless the local body lowers its budget.

What the records do not show

No record we found says a city, county or district raised a tax because the state cut income taxes. Income tax is a state tax; cutting it doesn't take money out of local budgets directly.
The property exemptions above do move costs to other local taxpayers, by the fiscal notes' own estimates. Those amounts are small per home.
How much growth sent to zones costs each county, school district or fire district has not been measured in the records reviewed.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

Your state representative and senatorWhen you give a local body the power to tax without a public vote, what notice do households get? Would you require a ballot question or a household cost estimate for new local sales taxes?
Your county commissionHow much of the new 0.2% sales tax goes to each purpose each year, and where is that reported? Was a ballot opinion question considered?
Your city council, school board and district boardsHow much property tax growth does your body share with PIDs, CRAs or the Inland Port, for how long, and what services does that growth area still need from you?
