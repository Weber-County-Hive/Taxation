The count

Our inventory holds 62 bills from 2015 through 2026 that changed Utah taxes or fees, or gave taxing authority to someone else. It includes rate increases, new taxes, fee changes, a change in who collects an existing tax, tax-increment arrangements, and one law later repealed. Each entry shows its status and what kind of change it made.

To pass, a bill needs a majority of all members of each house: at least 38 of 75 in the House and 15 of 29 in the Senate (Utah Const. Art. VI, Sec. 22). Those are the minimums, not each bill's tally. Each bill's own vote totals are listed where we found them recorded.

Many bills do more than one thing. Each bill is counted once, in its main group, and its other changes are listed in the "Also" line.

This is a floor, not a ceiling. Another 62 possible bills turned up that we could not confirm from a source we opened. They are listed under Not yet counted with the reason for each, and they are not in the totals. Small fee changes inside the yearly budget acts are not counted either.

A tax increase is not automatically good or bad. Several of these paid for roads, emergency services or school equity, and some were part of bills that cut other taxes. This page counts them and shows the record. It does not grade them.

Year by year

The Legislature's own increases were heaviest from 2017 to 2020. From 2021 to 2024 it raised almost no state taxes itself, but gave more taxing power to local bodies. In 2025 and 2026 it did both.

How a tax goes up in Utah

There are three routes in this count. The first two are the Legislature voting a state tax or fee. The third is the Legislature giving another body the power, and that body deciding later, sometimes with a local vote or hearing and sometimes without one.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

Your state representative and senatorWhich of these bills did you vote for, and why? When a zone keeps tax growth for 25 to 40 years, who checks whether the promised benefit happened?
Your county auditor or treasurerHow much of our county's property tax growth goes to zones, community reinvestment agencies and districts each year, and for how long?
Your city councilHas our city created a housing and transit zone, a first-home zone, a home-ownership zone or a reinvestment area? What does it keep, and when does it end?
The Legislative Fiscal AnalystIs there one yearly report that totals the tax growth kept by every zone and district in the state?
