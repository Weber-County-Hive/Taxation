How it works

The Legislature passes a lawIt sets what a local body may tax, how much, and whether voters must approve
▼
A local body decides whether to use itA city council, county commission, school board, district board or state authority board
▼
1. A new local sales or excise taxPaid at the register or on a utility bill. No individual notice to each household. A public vote only if the law requires one.
2. A property tax increaseAbove the certified rate, the body must mail notice and hold a Truth in Taxation hearing. Voters can only reverse it by referendum petition.
3. Tax growth sent to a zoneA share of future property tax growth goes to a zone, PID, CRA or authority instead of the county, schools and districts. No rate goes up, so no Truth in Taxation notice.
▼
You pay

Path 3 doesn't raise anyone's rate. It sends growth that the county, school district and other bodies would otherwise receive to a project. Those bodies still serve the new growth, so the cost can show up later as a rate increase or as less service. The records reviewed don't measure that for any one project.

TIF, CRA, PID, IFD and the zones: every tool that keeps tax growth

All of these use tax increment financing (TIF) or a special levy. With TIF, the property value in an area is frozen at a starting year. As the area grows, part of the new property tax goes to the project, not to the county, schools, fire and water districts that would otherwise get it. None of these tools raises anyone's rate, so none triggers a Truth in Taxation notice. A PID or IFD can add its own levy or assessment, paid by property owners inside its boundary.
